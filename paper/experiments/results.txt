EXPERIMENT 3 -- what FCA adds: the Dedekind-MacNeille completion
========================================================================
OntoDAG nodes (excluding the root)      : 11
Concepts of K(G) = (nodes, nodes, reach): 17

Every OntoDAG node appears in the completion as a concept whose
intent is its own reflexive ancestor set. The EXTRA concepts are
the meets the DAG never named:
  + unnamed meet: everything under {Booked, Flight}
                  = {inbound.pdf, outbound.pdf, paris-hop.pdf}
  + unnamed meet: everything under {Booked, Japan}
                  = {inbound.pdf, kyoto-inn.pdf, outbound.pdf, rail-pass.pdf}
  + unnamed meet: everything under {Booked, Japan, Refundable}
                  = {kyoto-inn.pdf, outbound.pdf, rail-pass.pdf}
  + unnamed meet: everything under {Booked, Flight, Japan}
                  = {inbound.pdf, outbound.pdf}

17 concepts = 11 that are OntoDAG nodes
                + 4 genuine unnamed meets
                + 2 bookends (the top and bottom a
                  complete lattice must have, even when nothing
                  in the data corresponds to them).

Each genuine meet is a combination of categories that happens to
be shared by a set of items, but that nobody asserted as a
category. FCA materialises them all; OntoDAG materialises none.

Written to figures/dm_completion.dot (asserted edges solid, the
meets the completion adds dashed).

========================================================================
EXPERIMENT 4 -- codes and DAGs are the same information
========================================================================
Claim (SEMANTIC_CODES.md Section 2): the family of reflexive
ancestor sets is a lossless, canonical re-encoding of the DAG.
Test: build random DAGs, take their codes, rebuild the covering
relation from the codes alone, and compare with the stored edges.

200/200 random DAGs: covering relation of code containment ==
the stored transitive reduction, edge for edge. The two directions
(DAG -> codes by ancestor closure, codes -> DAG by covering) are
mutually inverse, so subsumption really is a subset test.

========================================================================
The contranominal scale N_n = ({1..n}, {1..n}, !=) is the standard
worst case: every attribute subset is closed, so the lattice has
2^n concepts for n objects and n attributes.

========================================================================
EXPERIMENT 6 -- OntoDAG is not closed under meets (on purpose)
========================================================================
Put AB under {A, B}. Then put X under {A, B} as well.
  cone(A)          = ['AB', 'X']
  cone(B)          = ['AB', 'X']
  cone(A) & cone(B)= ['AB', 'X']
  cone(AB)         = []

So cone(AB) is a STRICT subset of cone(A) & cone(B): X is under
both A and B but not under AB. In lattice terms AB is a *sibling*
of the meet, never the meet itself. A query planner that
substituted AB for the pair {A, B} would silently lose X.
In FCA the meet always exists and is the concept with intent
closure({A,B}); in OntoDAG a named node is only ever what somebody
said it was.

EXPERIMENT 7 -- one canonical form, whatever the history
========================================================================

The single canonical edge set:

    kyoto-inn.pdf  ->  Hotel
    kyoto-inn.pdf  ->  Japan
    outbound.pdf  ->  Japan
    Flight  ->  Travel

dropped it, because Flight -> Travel already implies it.

========================================================================
EXPERIMENT 8 -- implications FCA would find in the same data
========================================================================
An attribute implication B -> C holds when every object with all
of B also has all of C. Here are the non-trivial ones with a
single-attribute premise, computed by closure:

  Flight      -> Booked    (support 3)
  Hotel       -> Booked, Japan, Refundable    (support 1)
  Refundable  -> Booked, Japan    (support 3)

Read as advice to the ontology: 'Flight -> Booked' says every
flight in this store happens to be booked. That is a fact about
today's population, not a rule -- filing one unbooked flight
destroys it. Section 8.1 of the paper is about exactly this
distinction, and why such implications belong in a derived,
local, regenerable layer rather than in the shared graph.

EXPERIMENT 9 -- an answer a stranger can check
========================================================================
root = d53e10b9ee4a06edac177b23d40a3ddab9a8f9a890a4397ac16f50548acc39ea
certificate format  : ontodag-is-below-certificate v1
claims              : outbound.pdf is below Travel -> True
records proved      : 4
registry pinned at  : 4.1

A verifier holding ONLY the root -- no store, no network -- checks:
  verify_below(cert, root) = True

Tampering is caught. Flip the claimed answer:
  refused: CertificateError

And a negative answer is provable too, in the same way:
  outbound.pdf below Hotel? False (verified: False)

This is what Section 10 means by 'trust nobody': the answer
travels, the store does not, and a wrong answer cannot verify.
